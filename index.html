🚀 Using this app

The three buttons

Tap Feed, Nappy or Sleep at the moment it happens. One tap is the whole job — the entry is saved straight away, and you can put the phone down.

Sleep is a toggle. Tap it when your baby goes down, and it changes to Wake up; tap it again when they wake, and the app works out how long they slept.

The card that appears after a tap

  • It shows when the next one is due, with a row of alternatives. Tapping one changes only that gap — useful for a long night stretch — and leaves your everyday plan alone.
  • After a nappy it also asks what was in it: wee, poo, both or dry. Answering is optional. Tap the same answer twice to clear it if you mis-tap.
  • It disappears on its own after twenty seconds, or with the ✕.

What's next

Predictions come from the intervals you set in Settings → Planned intervals, three hours by default. That means they match your routine from the very first entry, rather than waiting for a pattern to emerge.

Each one also shows what is actually happening. If it says actually averaging 4h 30m, your real routine has drifted from the plan — change the plan, or don't; the app is not going to nag.

Fixing mistakes

  • Tap any entry in the history to change its type, time or details.
  • Deleting gives you seven seconds to undo.
  • Add or edit an entry lets you record something you forgot, at the time it really happened.

History and measurements

History groups entries by day. Today and yesterday are open; older days are one tap away. Each day header totals that day's feeds, nappies — including wet and dirty counts — and sleep.

Measurements holds weight, length and temperature. They are not on the big buttons because they are not things you log half asleep. Weight shows in kilograms and in pounds and ounces, with the change since last time and against birth weight.

⚠ Keep a backup

Everything is stored in this browser and nowhere else. There is no account and no server — which is why nothing about your baby ever leaves your phone, and equally why clearing your browser data or changing phone will destroy it all.

Every so often: Settings → Export & backup → Download JSON. Restoring from that file brings back your entries, your planned intervals and the date of birth.

Restoring merges rather than replaces, so you can send the file to another phone and import it there. Each entry remembers when it last changed: the newer version of an entry wins, deletions carry across, and importing an out-of-date file cannot undo newer work.

Putting it on your home screen

On iPhone, open the app in Safari, tap the share button, then Add to Home Screen. It then opens like an ordinary app, full screen, and keeps working without a signal.

💡 For new parents

Reading your baby's sleep

Newborns spend about half their sleep in active sleep, and it can look alarming when you don't know what you're seeing.

  • Active sleep (REM). Eyelids flutter, the face makes grimaces and half-smiles, arms and legs twitch, breathing is irregular, and there are little grunts and squeaks. Your baby is asleep. Picking them up now often wakes them properly — most babies settle again on their own.
  • Quiet sleep. The body is still, the face relaxed, breathing steady and even. This is the deep phase, and they are much harder to wake.

Sleep cycles are short — roughly 40 to 50 minutes — so babies surface frequently. That is normal, not a sign anything is wrong.

Checking they're breathing — without waking them

  • Watch the chest and tummy rise and fall for half a minute. This is usually all you need, and it wakes nobody.
  • If you want more reassurance, warm your hand first, then rest it very lightly on their chest or back. A cold hand or firm pressure will wake them.
  • Do it during quiet sleep if you can. In active sleep the twitching and uneven breathing can worry you for no reason.

What's normal for a newborn: around 30 to 60 breaths a minute, and an uneven rhythm — a few quick breaths, then a pause of up to about 5 seconds, then breathing resumes. This is called periodic breathing and it settles as they grow.

⚠ When to call 999 straight away

  • A pause in breathing longer than 20 seconds, or any pause where they go pale, blue, grey or floppy
  • Blue or grey lips, tongue or face
  • Struggling to breathe: nostrils flaring, grunting with every breath, or the skin sucking in under and between the ribs
  • Limp, unresponsive, or very difficult to wake
  • A temperature of 38°C or higher in a baby under 3 months

For anything that worries you but isn't an emergency, call NHS 111, or speak to your midwife, health visitor or GP. You know your baby — if something feels wrong, get it checked.

Safer sleep basics

  • On their back for every sleep, day and night
  • Feet at the foot of the cot, with blankets tucked no higher than the shoulders
  • A firm, flat mattress. No pillows, duvets, cot bumpers or soft toys
  • Room temperature between 16°C and 20°C, with light layers or a sleeping bag. No hat indoors
  • Sleep in the same room as your baby for the first 6 months
  • Never sleep with your baby on a sofa or an armchair
  • Keep the room and the car smoke-free

This follows the guidance from The Lullaby Trust and the NHS, which are worth reading in full.

Feeds and nappies, roughly

  • Newborns commonly feed 8 to 12 times in 24 hours, and rarely on a tidy schedule. Several feeds bunched together in the evening is normal.
  • From about day 5, six or more properly wet nappies a day is a good sign they're getting enough.
  • Steady weight gain and wet nappies tell you far more than any clock does.

The predictions in this app are simply an average of what your baby has already done — they are a pattern, not a schedule. Feed your baby when they ask, not when the countdown says so. This page is general information, not medical advice.

Settings

👶 Baby

Used to show your baby's age, to work out weight change since birth, and to pick the right temperature threshold — under 3 months it is lower.

⏱ Planned intervals

How often you plan each one to happen. Predictions are built from these, so they match your routine from the very first entry rather than waiting for a pattern to emerge.

These are your everyday defaults. After logging something you can set a different gap just for that one time — handy for a long night stretch.

💾 Export & backup

Everything is stored in this browser only. Clearing site data or changing phone will delete it, so save a backup now and then. Restoring adds entries to what you already have and skips anything it has seen before.
